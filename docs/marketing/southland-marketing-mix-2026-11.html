Southland Organics · Channel plan · v2, 2026-11-17

Southland Marketing Mix

Phase 1 is backyard poultry only: fix the existing egg-laying content journey, get free Shopping listings in place, and run paid tests only once three launch gates pass. Commercial poultry, lawn and septic are mapped in Part 2 as future architecture. The channels there are hypotheses to test, not settled rules.

v2 revises v1 after an outside review. Changes: Phase 1 is split from the long-term map; fixed budget percentages are replaced with per-experiment caps; the Google bidding guidance is corrected; MER is no longer treated as proof that ads caused sales; and claims approval is now a launch gate.

Method

No plan here rests on past platform ROAS, because platform-attributed results are correlation. Two things replace it:

  • How each segment buys: who decides, where the order is placed, how often it repeats, and how big it is.
  • Controlled experiments measured against Southland's own order system (Nexus). Using Nexus data instead of platform data doesn't by itself settle cause and effect. Only a properly powered experiment does.

Part 1 · Act now

Phase 1: backyard poultry

Backyard poultry is the one segment where the whole loop happens online: discovery, purchase and reorder. Lawn, septic and commercial work wait until Phase 1 produces a result.

Launch gates (all three before any paid spend)

  1. Claims sheet. List approved, product-specific claims for each problem the ads will name (laying, mites, vent gleet), including any organic-status claims for poultry use. A customer problem can be a good topic without showing that a product treats its cause. Landing pages must match the approved claims.
  2. Contribution economics. Set the maximum cost to acquire a new customer from first-order contribution plus observed repeat contribution, not from revenue.
  3. Test feasibility. Run a power analysis on Nexus backyard order volume and variance to show what size of effect a holdout could detect.

Work that needs no media budget (start now)

  • Egg-laying content journey. This existing traffic should lead to a clear, claims-approved next step toward Hen Helper.
  • Free Merchant Center listings and feed quality. Clean titles, categories and attributes for the backyard products.
  • Retention. Post-purchase email and a consumables subscription in Customer.io.
  • Post-purchase survey. One to three questions that record both first touch and most influential touch.

Three selling motions

  • Distribution-led selling (commercial poultry). IPPE (Jan 26–28, 2027), dealer enablement (case-study sheets, rep training, co-op), farm radio, and the poultry biosecurity YouTube channel. Growers rank farm radio, dealers and peers highly as information sources (NAFB/Kynetec 2025, an industry-commissioned study). That shows where they listen, not that Meta can't work. Any Meta test here is measured on qualified opportunities.
  • Demand capture (Google).
    • Search built on named problems, matching the content hubs.
    • Standard Shopping, which has competed on Ad Rank with PMax since October 2024.
    • PMax as a challenger. The ~60 conversions a month and 10–25%-of-spend figures are third-party test assumptions (Optmyzr), not Southland findings.
    • For commercial leads, bid on qualified leads uploaded through enhanced conversions for leads.
  • Direct-response ecommerce (Meta). One acquisition campaign per segment at a time, with problem-led creative backed by the claims sheet. Creative volume and partnership-ad share are production choices to test. The 45% partnership-ad lift claim is unverified.

Budget is planned in three separate buckets: (1) existing commercial commitments and dealer support; (2) content, retention, feed and measurement; (3) incremental acquisition experiments, each with its own cap and decision date.

Google bidding

  • Google's Target ROAS page lists "at least 15 conversions in the past 30 days at the conversion tracking level" for Search and Shopping. Google's page does not say "per campaign" (v1's wording) or "per Merchant Center ID". Campaign splits still reduce the volume each bid strategy learns from, so keep structure lean.
  • Bidding is chosen per campaign type from Google's guidance for that type. No single low-volume default applies across Search and Shopping.

Open items

  • Offline conversion uploads. Google is moving offline uploads to the Data Manager API. Audit upload processing and conversion diagnostics before concluding anything is broken; legacy access may still apply.
  • Comparable brands. What Espoma, Jobe's and Manna Pro currently run hasn't been verified.
  • Dealer count. The site claims 500 dealers; the locator data has 114.
