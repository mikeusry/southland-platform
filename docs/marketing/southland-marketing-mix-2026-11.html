Southland Marketing Mix

Phase 1 spends nothing on paid acquisition for backyard poultry. A quart of Hen Helper sells for about $17 and lasts a small flock 4–5 months, so no ad channel can pay to win that customer. Phase 1 works on what changes that math: bigger packs, a reorder email timed to the bottle, the content journey, free Shopping listings and retention. Paid backyard tests come back only once the entry purchase is a gallon or a case.

v3 replaces v2's backyard paid experiments. The reason is product arithmetic, not past ad results: pack size and dose rate set what one purchase cycle is worth. Commercial poultry, lawn and septic stay in Part 2 as future architecture.

Method

No recommendation here rests on past platform ROAS, which is correlation. The plan is built from three things:

  • How each segment buys: who decides, where the order is placed, how often it repeats, and how big it is.
  • Product arithmetic: pack size divided by dose rate gives how long one purchase lasts, and that sets what a customer is worth per cycle.
  • Controlled experiments measured against Southland's own order system (Nexus). These come later, and only for channels whose arithmetic can work.

Prices and revenue shares are from Nexus order lines, 2026-01-15 to 2026-07-29. Duration is the dose arithmetic: 1 tablespoon per gallon of drinking water, with treated water changed every 2–3 days.

The quart is the best-selling size by units and brings in 8% of Hen Helper's revenue. A shopper who arrives with a problem buys the smallest size that solves it today, and that quart then lasts four months. Any acquisition cost has to be paid back out of about $17 of revenue per four-month cycle. Changing the ad channel doesn't change that math. Changing the pack size does.

Phase 1: backyard poultry, no paid spend

  1. Pack size.
    • Make the 1 gallon the default backyard size and the quart the trial size.
    • Build a seasonal backyard multi-gallon pack, mirroring how Big Ole Bird sells to commercial growers.
    • Frame it as "a season of flock care", not as a discount.
  2. Reorder email timed to the bottle.
    • Quart buyers get the reminder at about 4 months, not 30 days. A 30-day reminder reaches someone holding a mostly full bottle.
    • Gallon buyers get theirs much later, scaled to the size they bought.
    • Built in Customer.io. The reminder email teaches the ACV rotation.
  3. Egg-laying content journey. Existing organic traffic on laying, shells and molt leads to Hen Helper, sized to the gallon. Symptom and triage searches are best served by these organic pages, not by paid search.
  4. Free Merchant Center listings and feed quality. Clean titles, sizes and attributes, so free Shopping listings show the gallon and the bundle.
  5. Backyard Poultry Bundle. A multi-product starter purchase with a bigger basket than a single quart. Make it more prominent in the journey.
  6. Post-purchase survey. One to three questions recording both first touch and most influential touch.

Already done (2026-11-17 to 2026-11-18)

  • live The Hen Helper page now lists the quart bottle, not 16 oz.
  • live Invented testimonials on the Hen Helper and Bundle pages are replaced with verified customer reviews, quoted word for word.
  • live The ACV rotation is now consistent on the Hen Helper, Bundle, ACV and dosing-guide pages: Hen Helper 3 days, 2 days plain water, Mother Load ACV 2 days, 2 days plain water, repeat.

When backyard paid ads come back

Only once the typical first backyard purchase is a gallon or larger. Even then, test with a geo holdout measured against Nexus orders before scaling.

Claims and wording

  • Hen Helper claims stand as written: stronger shells, more eggs, better nutrient absorption. It is the same formula as Big Ole Bird, with probiotics and electrolytes.
  • Organic wording: "approved for use by some certifiers." Never "certified organic" or "USDA Organic".
  • Testimonials come only from verified customer reviews.
  • No competitor names. No free-shipping offers.

Three selling motions

  • Distribution-led selling (commercial poultry). IPPE (Jan 26–28, 2027), dealer enablement (case-study sheets, rep training, co-op), farm radio, and the poultry biosecurity YouTube channel. Big Ole Bird already sells in multi-gallon packs to this buyer, which is the model Phase 1 copies for backyard.
  • Demand capture (Google).
    • Search on named problems.
    • Standard Shopping.
    • PMax only as a challenger.
    • Commercial leads bid on qualified leads uploaded from Nexus.
    • Bidding is set per campaign type, from Google's guidance for that type.
  • Direct-response ecommerce (Meta). Only for segments where one purchase cycle can pay for acquisition. One campaign per segment at a time, judged by a holdout test.

Budget is planned in three buckets: (1) commercial commitments and dealer support; (2) content, retention, feed and measurement; (3) acquisition experiments, each with its own cap and decision date.
