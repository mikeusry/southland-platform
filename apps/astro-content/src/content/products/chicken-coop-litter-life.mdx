## Why Your Coop Smells Like Ammonia

Chicken droppings are full of uric acid. Bacteria in damp bedding turn that uric acid into ammonia, and that's the sharp smell you get when you open the coop. In winter, when the coop is closed up and ventilation drops, it builds up faster.

Adding fresh shavings on top hides the smell for a few days. It doesn't stop the bacteria underneath from making more.

## Fix It at the Source

Litter Life adds beneficial microbes, organic acids, and biologically active carbon to the coop floor. The good microbes outcompete the bacteria that make ammonia, and the bedding dries out and breaks down the way it's supposed to. Your coop smells better because less ammonia is forming, not because it's covered up.

## Simple to Use

Mix 1 pint of Litter Life with a gallon of water in a garden pump sprayer and spray the bedding. That covers about 250 square feet. Your hens can stay in the coop. Repeat every 2 to 4 weeks, or whenever the smell comes back.
