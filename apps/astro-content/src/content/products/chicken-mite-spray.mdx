## Treat the Coop and the Bird — Then Do It Again

Mites live in two places: on your chickens and in the coop. Northern fowl mites stay on the bird. Red mites hide in cracks and come out at night. If you only treat one place, the other one reinfests the flock.

1. **Treat the coop.** Clean out old bedding, then spray roosts, nest boxes, walls, floors, and corners. Mix 6 oz per ½ gallon of water; one batch covers about 250 sq ft.
2. **Treat the birds.** Mix 3 oz per ½ gallon of water. Brush the feathers up and mist down to the skin under the wings, around the vent, and at the base of the feathers.
3. **Repeat in 7–10 days.** Mite eggs hatch in 5–7 days. The second round kills them before they can lay eggs of their own.

## How to Tell If Your Chickens Have Mites

Check the coop at night with a flashlight, when red mites come out to feed — run a hand along the roost bars, and if it comes back streaked with what looks like dried blood, you have red mites. In daylight, part the feathers around the vent, under the wings, and on the breast and look for moving specks or dark debris at the feather bases. Other signs: feather loss around the vent, pale combs and wattles, fewer eggs, blood spots on eggs, and hens that won't go into the coop at night.
