## Built by athletes who lived in this gear

JockShock was built by people who actually used it first — D1 collegiate athletes who lived through the gear bags, the locker rooms, the smell that stays in the car after practice. The product exists because the founders couldn't find a deodorizer that was strong enough to matter, safe enough to use on every piece of gear, and built for an after-every-practice routine instead of occasional damage control.

## The chemistry: hypochlorous acid, not fragrance

Most "gear sprays" mask odor with fragrance. JockShock kills the odor at its source.

The active is **hypochlorous acid (HOCl)** — powered by EPA-registered ZeroPoint Technology. That is not a marketing name for something exotic. HOCl is the same molecule your own white blood cells make. When a neutrophil engulfs something during the immune response, an enzyme called myeloperoxidase converts chloride into hypochlorous acid — the oxidant your body has used for this job for as long as you have had an immune system. It is also the active in the saline wound sprays sold behind the pharmacy counter.

That is why JockShock is safe on skin contact at use concentration and still does real work on a helmet liner.

## Why gear smells in the first place

Sweat itself is close to odorless. The smell comes from what happens to sweat afterward: bacteria that live on skin and in gear metabolize it into small, extremely pungent volatile compounds — thioalcohols, which read as onion and sulfur, and isovaleric acid, which is the sharp cheese-and-cleat note that comes off a shoe.

This is why fragrance-based sprays fail in a predictable way. Perfume adds a smell on top of a smell. The volatile compounds are still there, still being produced, and the cover-up wears off before the source does. Going after the source is a different mechanism, not a stronger version of the same one.

## What "powered by ZeroPoint" means

ZeroPoint is Southland Organics' own hypochlorous acid technology, and it carries an EPA-registered label. JockShock is that chemistry, put into a 32 oz bottle with a ratchet-pistol trigger and built for athletic gear rather than a barn.

To be precise about it: the EPA registration belongs to **ZeroPoint**. JockShock is powered by it. That distinction is why this page says "powered by EPA-registered ZeroPoint Technology" and not "JockShock is EPA-registered" — the first is true, the second is not, and we would rather be exact than impressive.

No fragrance. No bleach. No masking.
