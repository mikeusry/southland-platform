## Why do football pads smell so bad?

Sweat itself is close to odorless. The stink is what happens next.

Bacteria that live on skin and in gear metabolize leftover sweat into small, extremely pungent volatile compounds. Two of them do most of the damage in a football bag:

- **Isovaleric acid** — the cheese-and-cleat note
- **Thioalcohols** — onion and sulfur

Shoulder pads, neck rolls, hip pads, and helmet liners are foam wrapped in fabric with straps that never fully dry in a closed bag. That is an incubator, not a laundry problem. Population doubles fast in warm, wet foam. One Friday night in the bag and Monday smells like a season.

This is why "I washed everything" still fails. You washed the jersey. The pads never went in the machine.

## Can you put football pads in the washing machine?

**No — not the shoulder pads.** The shell, foam, and hardware are not built for a spin cycle. You will wreck the pad before you fix the smell.

What you *can* wash:

- Practice jerseys, girdles, socks, towels — regular laundry, no fabric softener (it traps odor in fibers)
- Removable helmet pads / liners, if the manufacturer says they come out
- Gloves, if they are machine-safe; if not, treat them like pads

What you treat in place:

- Shoulder pads
- Hip and thigh pads that stay in the girdle
- Helmet interior (liner, cheek pads, chinstrap)
- The inside of the gear bag

USA Football and most equipment managers land in the same place: wipe, air-dry, do not soak the foam. The missing piece in that advice is *what chemistry actually goes after the odor molecules sitting in the foam*, versus perfume sprayed on top of them.

## Why don't Febreze, dryer sheets, and "gear spray" fix it?

Fragrance adds a smell on top of a smell. The volatile compounds are still there, still being produced, and the cover-up wears off before Friday.

Bleach and harsh peroxide sprays are the other common miss. They are the wrong category for gear you put on a body. They can also oxidize fabric and foam in ways you do not want on a $400 set of pads.

Sunlight and a garage hook help — they dry the pad, which slows the chemistry. Dry pads still hold last week's isovaleric acid in the foam. Drying is necessary. It is not sufficient.

## How do I clean a football helmet without wrecking it?

Do not dunk it. Do not run it through a dishwasher.

- Wipe the shell with a damp cloth.
- Mist the liner, cheek pads, and chinstrap with JockShock.
- Let it dry fully before the next wear.
- If the manufacturer allows removable liners to come out, pull them, mist, air-dry, snap back.

The smell in a helmet is the same isovaleric-acid story as the shoulder pads. Same chemistry, tighter space.

## What about the gear bag?

The bag is not innocent. It holds yesterday's air and yesterday's sweat. Unzip it. Invert it. Mist the interior. Let it stand open overnight. If you only treat the pads and zip them back into a sour bag, you re-inoculate the foam on the ride home.
