People who are deciding on [Torched](/products/torched-all-natural-weed-killer/) usually ask the same thing: _is this just vinegar in a nicer jug?_

No. They do the same _kind_ of job. They are not the same product.

## Torched vs 30% vinegar

Both are **contact** killers. They burn the leaf they hit. They do not travel to the root the way a systemic chemical does. Young annual weeds often die in one pass. Deep-rooted perennials need more than one.

That is the comparison. Not a kill-rate contest. We do not have a head-to-head trial, and we will not invent one.

**What is different:**

- **What it is.** Torched is clove oil, cornmint oil, and soap. Horticultural vinegar is acetic acid — often 20–30%. Grocery vinegar is about 5% and is too weak for this job.
- **How you use it.** Torched is a concentrate. Mix 8–10 oz per gallon (10–12 for hardy or mature weeds), shake, spray. Vinegar at horticultural strength is harsh to handle. You wear protection for a reason.
- **How it smells.** Reviewers who have used both say Torched smells better. That is their words, not a lab result.

Do not buy Torched if what you wanted was a jug of acetic acid at a lower price. Buy it if you want a mixed, plant-oil contact spray that you can use around a coop, a driveway, or a bed without leaving a soil residue.

## Is it organic certified?

**No.** Torched is not USDA Organic.

The label says all-natural. That is not a certification.

It is classified by the EPA as a **minimum-risk pesticide (FIFRA 25b exempt)** — clove and mint oil on the minimum-risk list. If you run a certified organic farm, ask your certifier. We cannot answer that for them.

## What it will and will not do

Torched burns the foliage it touches. It does **not** attack the root system. It does **not** leave a residue that stops the next seed. New weeds in living soil a couple of weeks later are new seeds, not the old plant "waking up" — unless you stopped after one pass on a perennial, in which case the root is spending reserves. Hit it again at 5–7 days.

It is non-selective. Flowers, food plants, and grass brown if you coat them. Shield what you want to keep. If you spray something by mistake, rinse it.

Kids, pets, and poultry can return when it is dry. We do not have a trial on horses with metabolic issues. Same rule: once dry.

Named weeds we will stand behind: dandelion, thistle, puncturevine, and established gravel-driveway weeds. We will not add names we have not earned. Bindweed has been reported as not working.
