## Farming is an act of faith.

"My name is Shannon, and this is my Southland story." He farms in Comer, Georgia, with his wife and two sons: about 42 acres, cattle, and five poultry houses. Both of his grandfathers farmed. Grandfather Hayes had an Allis-Chalmers tractor dealership — that's where Shanon learned to run a tractor and a plow.

After he and his wife married, they looked at farms and the timing wasn't right. He felt called to ministry and pastored instead. Years later, driving the country road he still lives on, he saw a sign on this place.

"I pulled in thinking that there's no way this could all work out. Just a few months later, I was a poultry farm."

The step was not small:

> I invested my family farm that my grandfather gave me. I put that whole farm up for collateral for this. I risked all of my inheritance. A farm my grandfather gave me to be able to purchase this, and so I put it all up for it. So it was a huge step of faith.

"Farming is almost an act of faith," he says. "You're just taking a chance on producing a good chicken. We put those chickens out in that chicken house that they're going to grow and they're going to live and come out a good way, and everything's going to work out good."

Has his faith been tested as a farmer? "Sure it has."

## Dead birds can kill a farm.

December 2014. About the fifth week of a flock, almost-full-grown birds started dying by the hundreds. Nobody had diagnosed it yet. They finished that flock, evaluated, and grew another. That one rocked along fine — until week five, when the same thing happened.

They finally determined it was dermatitis in the house. People said try one more flock. He was a young poultry farmer who had never seen anything like it. He said okay. The same thing happened again.

> There's no way. I can't raise one more flock in this house. We cannot financially make it. I can't mentally make it. Something's got to give here. I was at panic mode.

The three dermatitis flocks ran about **8%, 7%, and 10% mortality** — all losing big birds. He had Allen Reynolds from Southland Organics on the phone about treating the floor. They cleaned out down to the dirt and treated heavily with [Litter Life](/products/poultry-litter-amendment/).

The next flock, around day 35, he found a few birds that looked like they might have something. The next morning, everything was clear. Mortality on that flock was right at **2%**. "I went from almost 10 percent to 2 percent mortality rate after treating the floors and cleaning out the house."

People had told him dermatitis is in the litter and in the dirt — that you can never really get it out of a house once it's had it. After Litter Life, **15 months** with no recurrence.

"I believe the Litter Life product helped. Southland has been exceptional. Getting the product to me when I need the product… I need it, I got it, and it's taken care of my birds and the farm."

The man he bought the farm from had told him there would be days he'd ask why he ever looked at a chicken farm, and other days he'd go out there and wouldn't do anything different. "And it has come true."
